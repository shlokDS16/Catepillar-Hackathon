## 0. Key framing: there are TWO distinct, unrelated Caterpillar competitions

Do not conflate these — they have different sponsors-within-Caterpillar, platforms, and purposes:

| | **Caterpillar Tech Challenge / "Tech-A-Thon"** | **Caterpillar Autonomy Challenge (CAC)** |
|---|---|---|
| Run by | Caterpillar India Engineering Solutions (Early Talent Hiring) | Caterpillar, via IIT Madras's Shaastra tech fest |
| Platform | Beamery (`pages.beamery.com/caterpillarinc/...`), pan-India open registration | Unstop, hosted as a Shaastra (IIT Madras) event |
| Format | Software/hardware prototype (vision, sensors, embedded, mechanical design) against Caterpillar-authored problem statements | Physical rover-building robotics competition (autonomous excavation/construction on simulated lunar terrain) |
| Scale | Pan-India, 600+ colleges, 13,000+ students (2025) | Single-event, IIT Madras Shaastra festival |
| Reward | Cash-equivalent goodies (₹1L/₹60k) + plant tour + possible future project involvement | Cash prize pool (₹3.5L) + PPI (Pre-Placement Interview) opportunities |

## 2. Problem statements (verified, full text) — most recent fully-documented set: Tech-A-Thon 2025

Retrieved directly from Caterpillar's official Beamery problem-statement page. [pages.beamery.com/caterpillarinc/tech-a-thon-25-problem-statements](https://pages.beamery.com/caterpillarinc/page/tech-a-thon-25-problem-statements-0c5cyvlog_)

**PS1 — Real-Time Monocular Depth Estimation on Edge AI**
- Eligible depts: ECE, EEE, Instrumentation, Robotics, AI/ML, Data Science, Data Analytics, IT, CS, Mechatronics
- Problem: predict per-pixel depth from a single RGB camera (no depth sensor) for 3D perception, autonomous driving, and Cat vehicle **collision mitigation systems**. Use KITTI/NYUv2 datasets; metric = Absolute Relative Distance Error. Must alert the operator visually (LED/display/audio) based on distance to cars/personnel.
- Deployment target: cheap edge-AI hardware — Raspberry Pi AI Kit, BeagleY-AI, TI SK-TDA4VM, NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super (or similar), not limited to these.
- **Winner: PSG Institute of Technology and Applied Research.**

**PS2 — Vision system to identify and display defects on components**
- Eligible depts: AI/Data Science, Mechanical, Production, Manufacturing, Aeronautical, Industrial, Automobile, Metallurgy, Mining, Mechatronics
- Problem: manual visual inspection of paint aesthetics, weld aesthetics, gap measurements is unrepeatable/error-prone. Build an AI vision system with a built-in deep-learning model to detect and display defects covering paint appearance, weld appearance, assembly quality, machining quality.
- **Winner: PSG College of Technology.**

**PS3 — DEF tank with mechanism to measure DEF quality & control DEF filling**
- Eligible depts: Mechanical, Production, Manufacturing, Aeronautical, Industrial, Automobile, Metallurgy, Mining, Mechatronics
- Problem: Diesel Exhaust Fluid (DEF/AUS32, ISO 22241-1, ~32.5% urea) is often adulterated in the market; existing DEF quality sensors detect contamination only *after* filling. Design a tank + mechanism that measures urea quality **before/during filling**, alerts the operator, has negligible impact on fill time/rate, and is structurally durable. Constraints: mass-producible, cost-effective, meets urea-filling regulations, standard filling-gun compatible. Deliverables: design + structural simulation + working prototype.
- **Winner: IIT Madras** (Innovative DEF Tank Design).

## 3. Caterpillar Autonomy Challenge (CAC) 2026 — Shaastra/IIT Madras — full rules extracted

Source: official CAC 2026 rulebook PDF. [cac.shaastra.org](https://cac.shaastra.org/) / [PDF](https://cac.shaastra.org/assets/CATERPILLAR%20AUTONOMY%20CHALLENGE_2026-Bo_P5MoA.pdf)

**Theme (2026 edition):** autonomous **lunar/off-world construction rovers** — "focus on autonomous capabilities and the construction skills of rovers designed for space exploration." Robots must navigate a simulated off-world arena, dig sand from an excavation zone, and construct **berms** (mounds) at a target construction zone.

**Format:** physical robotics competition, not a software-only prototype.
- Arena: ~9m x 5m, filled with ~30cm sand, containing an obstacle zone (boulders, craters, a central support column), an excavation zone, and a construction/target zone (berm target size 1.5m x 0.9m).
- Robot constraints: max 80kg mass, must fit a 1.5m x 0.75m x 1m payload envelope (may expand to 1.75m height after start); no GPS/IMU-GPS, no compasses, no wall-based navigation/mapping.
- **Autonomy tiers (teams choose min. one level, higher tiers = higher score):**
  - Excavation/Dump Automation — 100 pts
  - Excavation & Dump Automation (full cycle, hands-free) — 200 pts
  - Travel Automation (hands-free crossing of obstacle zone) — 300 pts
  - Full Autonomy (≥2 full excavate-transport-place cycles, entirely hands-free) — 600 pts
- Scoring formula: Berm Construction Productivity normalized by robot mass (BCP-mass) and by energy consumed (BCP-energy) — cubic cm of berm built per minute per kg, and per watt-hr of energy (measured via a mandatory COTS data logger). Camera-usage penalty (0 cams=120pts, 1 cam=60pts, 2 cams=0pts, penalty beyond 2 cams).
- Control room simulates a "Lunar In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) construction mission" — max 5 team members, no faculty, hands-free autonomy declarations required, judges are observers only (no mid-run help).
- Navigation aids: markers (ArUco/AprilTags/QR) and/or beacons (LED/IR/low-power radio/Class I-II laser), max combined 2 beacons + 3 markers, must be detected/interpreted fully onboard (no human-in-the-loop reading of markers).
- Communications: IEEE 802.11 Wi-Fi only, two IP-configured routers provided (no ethernet), Bluetooth allowed only at low power classes, Zigbee/802.15.4 banned. All wireless/laser equipment must comply with **India's WPC (Wireless Planning & Coordination) regulations and BIS safety norms** — a distinctly India-context regulatory requirement.
- Safety: mandatory unmodified COTS E-STOP button (min 40mm), lithium-ion battery handling protocol, "Proof of Life" video deliverable required (no points, but disqualifying if not submitted; submission mechanism to be announced via **Unstop**).
- Each team gets 2 competition attempts; cumulative (not best-of) scoring.

**Eligibility/registration:** via Unstop; 2026 registration deadline **14 August 2026** (per earlier search result) — this has already passed as of today (23 Sept 2026), meaning **CAC 2026 registration is closed; the finale/competition itself likely occurs at Shaastra 2026** (IIT Madras's annual fest — exact Shaastra 2026 dates not confirmed in this research; Shaastra typically runs January). **UNVERIFIED**: exact CAC 2026 event/finale date.

**Prizes:** cash prize pool reported at ₹3.5 lakh plus **Pre-Placement Interview (PPI)** opportunities with Caterpillar. [shareyaarnow.com](https://www.shareyaarnow.com/caterpillar-autonomy-challenge/33732/) — note this is a secondary/lower-tier source; treat prize figure as UNVERIFIED pending confirmation from cac.shaastra.org itself (the official site's homepage returned minimal scrapable text).

**Distinct prior instance:** a 2024 "Caterpillar Industry Defined Problem" existed at Shaastra 2024 on Unstop, suggesting Caterpillar has run some form of IIT-Madras-hosted challenge annually since at least 2024, evolving into the dedicated "Caterpillar Autonomy Challenge" branding by 2025–2026. [unstop.com](https://unstop.com/competitions/industry-defined-problem-shaastra-2024-iit-madras-825769)

## 6. Flags / contradictions of conventional hackathon assumptions

1. **No live/open Caterpillar student hackathon exists right now (23 Sept 2026).** Tech Challenge 2026 concluded; CAC 2026 registration (deadline 14 Aug 2026) has closed. If the goal is to compete, the actionable near-term target is **watching for Tech Challenge 2027 launch (~Feb 2027)** or a **VIT-specific edition** (VIT ran one in 2024; pattern suggests recurrence is plausible but not confirmed for 2026/2027).
2. **Two unrelated "Caterpillar hackathons" exist** (Tech Challenge/Tech-A-Thon vs. Autonomy Challenge) plus a third unrelated **workforce-development competition** ($1M, non-student, nonprofit/company-focused) — easy to conflate in search results; verify which one any new lead refers to.
3. **PPO is not explicitly promised anywhere** for the Tech Challenge — only "potential involvement in future projects." The Autonomy Challenge (CAC) is the one that explicitly names **PPI**, not the pan-India Tech Challenge. Don't assume the flagship Tech Challenge guarantees an interview.
4. **Problem statements rotate per edition and are department-gated** (e.g., PS1 requires ECE/EEE/AI-ML/CS/Mechatronics background; PS2/PS3 require Mechanical/Production/Automobile background) — teams cannot freely pick any problem statement regardless of major; eligibility is enforced per-department.
5. **CAC is a hardware/robotics competition**, not a software-prototype hackathon — building expectations (data loggers, E-STOP buttons, lithium battery protocols, India WPC/BIS regulatory compliance) are industrial-safety-grade, unusually rigorous for a "student hackathon."
